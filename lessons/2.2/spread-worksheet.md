# Worksheet: two symbols, two spreads

Do this on your own **paper** account, with the columns from `quote-columns.md` in a watchlist.

1. Pick **one busy fund** (like SPY) and **one stock that trades much less**.
2. For each, write down the bid, the ask, the spread in cents, and the volume.
3. Turn each spread into a percent of the price:

   **spread % = spread ÷ price × 100**

   (For the price, the ask or the midpoint between bid and ask both work: the difference is tiny.)

| | Symbol | Bid | Ask | Spread (cents) | Price | Spread (%) | Volume |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Busy fund | | | | | | | |
| Quieter stock | | | | | | | |

## Worked example (the lesson's numbers)

| | Symbol | Spread | Price | Spread (%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Busy fund | IBIT | 1 cent | about $46 | 0.01 ÷ 46 × 100 ≈ **0.02 %** |
| Quieter stock | IBKR | 27 cents | about $84 | 0.27 ÷ 84 × 100 ≈ **0.32 %** |

More exactly, 0.0217 % against 0.32 %: 0.32 ÷ 0.0217 ≈ 15, roughly fifteen times as much, every time you cross the
spread (buy at the ask, sell at the bid).
For a day trader who trades often, that adds up fast.

## Check yourself

- The busy fund should show a spread of a cent or two: a tiny fraction of a percent.
- The quieter stock shows more cents, and a bigger percent.
- If your prices sit on an olive background, they are delayed, just like the lesson's.

These symbols are examples of a thick and a thinner market, not recommendations.
